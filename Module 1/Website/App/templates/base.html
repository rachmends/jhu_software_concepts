Applied Mathematics MS Student at Johns Hopkins University

Welcome to my Quant Research Space!

Here we like to have a little fun (mathematical fun that is).

I earned my bachelor's degree in chemical engineering from Cornell University! Go Big Red!

Whilst at Cornell, I worked as an undergraduate researcher in Professor Brett P. Fors’ laboratory at Cornell University. My research initially focused on reversible addition–fragmentation chain-transfer (RAFT) polymerizations, where I gained experience with polymer synthesis and characterization. I later worked on nanoscale polymerizations on silicon wafers, exploring polymer growth and surface-based materials synthesis.

I have extensive internship experience in pharmaceuticals (Merck) and energy (Duracell). I worked in polymer manufacturing at ExxonMobil for approximately two years following my undergraduate graduation. During this venture into chemical engineering fieldwork, I grew more attached to data analysis than the process/physical mechanic configurations of manufacturing (reactors, heat exchangers, compressors, extrusion panels, etc.).

As a result, for my graduate coursework and desire to pivot into a more data analytical role, I am seeking an advanced degree in applied mathematics from Johns Hopkins University.

My primary academic interest is therefore differential equations and mathematical modeling, particularly in applications to physical and quantum chemistry, physics and astrophysics, electromagnetism, and optics and light-wave propagation. I am interested in starting with a physical phenomenon or problem, determining how its relevant principles can be expressed mathematically, and developing differential-equation-based models that can describe its behavior, reveal underlying mechanisms, or help identify ways of addressing the problem.
